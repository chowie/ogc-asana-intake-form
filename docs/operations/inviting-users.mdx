Accounts are invitation-only. Only **inviters** can send invitations. An
inviter is anyone whose email is listed in the `INVITER_EMAILS` environment
variable.

## Sending an invite

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in as an inviter">
    Inviters don't need an invitation themselves. Signing in for the first time
    creates an active account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Invite panel">
    Click **Invite** in the header, next to your name, **Profile**, **Users**,
    and **Sign out**. Non-inviters don't see this button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter their name and email">
    **First name** and **Last name** are required. They become the person's
    account name (they can correct the spelling on their first sign-in) and
    personalize the invite email. Then type the address they'll register with.
    It has to match exactly when they register (capital letters don't matter),
    so if they'll use **Sign up with Google**, enter their Google address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose how to deliver it">
    **Email the invite to them for me** is checked by default. With it checked,
    they get an email with the subject "*Your name* invited you to the OGC
    Deacon Board request form". It opens "Hi *first name*,", names you as
    "*Your name* (*your email*)" so it's clearly from a person, and has an
    **Accept invite** button. Clear the checkbox to skip the email and send the link yourself by
    text, chat, or your own email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create invite">
    The panel shows the link (`…/invite?token=…`) with a **Copy** button and
    the expiry time. The link is shown even when it was emailed. If the email
    fails to send, the panel says so, and you can copy the link and send it
    yourself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Every invite link:

- works **once**,
- works **only for the email you entered**, and
- expires after **48 hours**.

The link is shown only in the panel when you create it. It isn't stored
anywhere you can look it up later. Redis keeps only a hash of it.

Creating an invite also creates the person's account record right away, with
status **Invited**, so it shows up in the [Users tray](#seeing-who-has-an-account)
even if they never accept.

<Note>
  You can't invite someone who already has an active account. You'll see
  "That person already has an account" and should tell them to sign in.
  There's a limit of 20 invites per hour per IP.
</Note>

## What the invitee sees

<Steps>
  <Step title="Accept your invitation">
    The link opens a **Register** screen. They enter the invited email, or use
    **Sign up with Google** with a Google account on that address. If the
    address doesn't match, they get an error and can try again. The invite
    isn't used up by a mismatch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your email">
    Next comes a **Confirm your email** screen with numbered steps and a
    **Resend confirmation email** button. Each confirmation link lasts **24
    hours**, but the account itself doesn't expire: it stays **Awaiting email
    confirmation** until they confirm, and Resend sends a fresh link any time.
    The app stays locked until they click it, even if they signed up with
    Google.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Signed in">
    Clicking **Confirm my email** on the page the link opens signs them in.
    After that they sign in with an emailed link or Google. See
    [Submitting a request](/staff/submitting-a-request#1-sign-in).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check their name">
    On their first sign-in they see **Welcome! Please check your details**,
    with the first and last name you entered. They fix any misspelling and
    press **Save and continue**. See [Your profile](/staff/your-profile).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Seeing who has an account

Click **Users** in the header (inviters only, next to **Invite**). A panel
slides in from the right listing every account, sorted by name:

| Shown | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Name and email | The account's first and last name (or the email, if it has no name) |
| Status badge | **Invited**, **Awaiting email confirmation**, **Active**, or **Disabled** — see below |
| Invited | When the latest invite was sent, and by whom. **—** if the account wasn't invited (for example, an inviter who bootstrapped by signing in) |
| Accepted | When they confirmed their email. **Never** if they haven't yet |
| Last login | Their most recent sign-in. **Never** if none is recorded |

<Note>
  Last login is recorded only from this release on. Someone who last signed
  in before then shows **Never** until their next sign-in. Older accounts'
  **Accepted** date comes from the confirmation time they already had on
  record.
</Note>

### Account statuses

| Status | Badge | What it means | Can sign in? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `invited` | Invited | An invite was created; they haven't registered | No |
| `pending` | Awaiting email confirmation | They registered with the invite but haven't clicked the confirmation link | No |
| `active` | Active | Confirmed (or an inviter's auto-created account) | Yes |
| `disabled` | Disabled | Access revoked by hand (see below) | No |

None of these expire. An `invited` record stays even after its 48-hour link
has lapsed, and a `pending` record stays until they confirm.

## Re-inviting

Create a new invite for the same email. This works for anyone **Invited** or
**Awaiting email confirmation**, and it refreshes their first and last name
and the invite date in the Users tray. Use it when:

- **The invite expired or was lost.** It's unused after 48 hours.
- **You misspelled their name.** Re-invite with the correct spelling (or let
  them fix it on their first sign-in).
- **They registered but closed the screen with the Resend button.** Their
  account is still pending, so a new invite lets them register again and get
  a fresh confirmation email. If they still have that screen open, **Resend
  confirmation email** is enough.
- **You mistyped their address.** Invite the correct address. The old invite
  simply expires, but its **Invited** record stays in the Users tray until you
  delete it (see [Fixing a record](#fixing-a-record)).

Old invites can't be cancelled, but they only work for the address they were
created for, and they expire on their own.

## Revoking access

Accounts are stored in Upstash Redis. The recommended way to remove someone is
to **disable** their record:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Data Browser">
    [console.upstash.com](https://console.upstash.com) → your production
    database → **Data Browser**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find their key">
    Search for `user:<their email, lowercased>`, for example
    `user:jane.doe@example.com`. The value looks like
    `{ "email": …, "firstName": …, "lastName": …, "status": "active", "provider": "email", "invitedBy": …, … }`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set status to disabled">
    Edit the value and change `"status": "active"` to `"status": "disabled"`.
    Leave the rest of the JSON as it is, and save.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Every authenticated request checks this record, so their next request returns
401 and drops them to the sign-in screen, even if a session is still open.
Sign-in links, Google sign-in, and old confirmation links all stop working for
them, and because the record still exists, they can't be re-provisioned
automatically.

**Deleting** the key also revokes a regular member immediately. Disabling is
still the better choice: it leaves a record of the revocation, and it also
works for inviters.

<Warning>
  Deleting an **inviter's** record isn't enough. With no record, an
  `INVITER_EMAILS` address is re-provisioned as active on its next sign-in. To
  revoke an inviter, either set their status to `"disabled"` (which takes
  effect immediately and holds even while they're still listed), or remove
  them from `INVITER_EMAILS`, redeploy, **and then** delete the record.
</Warning>

To restore someone, set their status back to `"active"` in the Data Browser.
Re-inviting won't do it: `create-invite` refuses a disabled account (409
"That account is disabled"), and an invite sent before they were disabled
is rejected at registration (403). A disabled account only comes back when
someone deliberately edits the record.

## Fixing a record

Most fixes don't need Redis. People correct their own name under **Profile**,
and most can change their own sign-in email there too (see
[Your profile](/staff/your-profile)). For anything else, edit the
`user:<email>` key in the Upstash Data Browser, as above:

- **Wrong name**: edit `firstName` / `lastName`. The header and future tasks
  use it on the next request.
- **Stale invite** (mistyped address, person no longer coming): delete the
  `user:<that address>` key to remove it from the Users tray. If the 48-hour
  link is still live it can still be used to register, which recreates the
  record.
- **Inviter's email**: inviters can't change their address in the app.
  Replace it in `INVITER_EMAILS` (see below). The new address gets a fresh
  account on its first sign-in; disable or delete the old record.

Leave `status`, the timestamps, and the other fields as they are unless you
mean to change them.

## Adding or removing inviters

Edit `INVITER_EMAILS` in **Netlify → Site configuration → Environment
variables**. It's a comma-separated list, and spaces and capitalization don't
matter.

<Warning>
  Netlify applies environment-variable changes to functions only on the **next
  deploy**. After editing, go to **Deploys → Trigger deploy → Deploy site**.
  Until that deploy finishes, the old list is still in effect.
</Warning>

- **Adding** someone lets them sign in without an invite once the deploy
  finishes, and the **Invite** button appears after they reload. If they
  already had an active account, it simply gains invite rights. A `disabled`
  record stays disabled. Set it back to `"active"` yourself.
- **Removing** someone takes away the Invite button and the ability to sign in
  without an account. Their account stays **active** as a normal member. To
  cut off their access completely, also disable their record (see above).

Invite rights are checked against `INVITER_EMAILS` on every request. They
aren't stored on the account.
