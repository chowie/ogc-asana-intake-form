The whole thing takes two or three minutes. Nothing is sent to the deacons until
you press **Confirm**, so there is no way to accidentally submit a half-finished
request.

## 1. Sign in

Each person has their own account, and there are no passwords. To get an
account you need an invitation from a deacon. If you don't have one yet, ask.

### Accepting your invitation (first time only)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the invite link">
    It arrives by email from the deacon who invited you ("*Their name* invited
    you to the OGC Deacon Board request form"), greets you by first name, and
    names that deacon and their email address. The deacon may also send you
    the link directly. It works **once**, only for **your** email address, and
    expires after **48 hours**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register">
    Type the email address the invite was sent to and press **Register**. You
    can also press **Sign up with Google** if your Google account uses that
    same address. If the address doesn't match, you'll see an error, and you
    can fix it and try again. The invite isn't used up by a mistake.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your email">
    You'll see a **Confirm your email** screen. Open the email from OGC Deacons
    (check spam or promotions), click **Confirm my email**, then press the
    confirm button on the page that opens. If it doesn't arrive, use **Resend
    confirmation email**. Each confirmation link works for **24 hours**. If
    yours expires, press **Resend confirmation email** for a fresh one. Your
    registration doesn't lapse, so you won't need a new invite.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check your name">
    Confirming signs you in. You can't use the form until this step is done,
    even if you signed up with Google. The first time you sign in you'll see
    **Welcome! Please check your details**, with your first and last name
    filled in from the invite. Fix any misspellings and press **Save and
    continue**. This is the name the deacons see on your requests. See
    [Your profile](/staff/your-profile).
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Signing in after that

Pick either option on the sign-in screen:

- **Email me a sign-in link.** Enter your email and open the link you're sent.
  It works once and expires in **15 minutes**. When the page opens, press
  **Sign in**.
- **Sign in with Google**, if it's shown.

<Note>
  Links from our emails always open a page with a button to press. That's on
  purpose: some email systems open links automatically to scan them, and the
  button keeps the scan from using up your link.
</Note>

You're signed out after **30 minutes without activity**, or when you close the
browser. If that happens while you're filling out a request, you'll see a
notice on the sign-in screen. **Your request is still there.** Sign in again
and submit it. If you open the sign-in link in a new tab, go back to your
original tab and it will continue where you were.

**Sign out** (top right) ends your session and clears the form.

## 2. Fill out the form

There's no name or email to fill in. Every request is recorded under your
account: the name on your [profile](/staff/your-profile) (or your email, if
you haven't set a name) and the email you signed in with. Your name appears
at the top right of the page; hover over it to see your email.

<ParamField path="Request Title" required>
  A short summary — this becomes the Asana task name. "Replace parking lot
  lights" beats "Question".
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Request Details" required>
  Describe the request. More detail here means fewer questions from Philip.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Due Date">
  Optional. Sets the due date on the Asana task.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Assign to">
  Optional. Assigns the task to a specific person. Leave it as **Unassigned**
  unless you know who should own it — the deacons triage the Inbox either way.
  The list includes everyone on the staff roster.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Supporting Document">
  Optional. PDF, Word, Excel, or image, **700 KB or smaller**. See
  [Attachments](/staff/attachments).
</ParamField>

## 3. Answer Philip's questions

After you submit, Philip reads your request and asks up to **three** short
questions — things like who the request is for, or when it needs to happen. If
your request is already clear, he skips straight to the summary.

Full detail on this step: [Working with Philip](/staff/working-with-philip).

## 4. Review the summary

Philip produces a five-part brief:

| Section | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **What** | The request itself |
| **Context** | Why it matters / background |
| **Scope** | What is and isn't included |
| **Constraints / timing** | Deadlines, budget, limitations |
| **Definition of done** | How everyone knows it's finished |

Two buttons:

- **Confirm** — creates the Asana task.
- **Edit** — sends you back to the form with your answers still filled in. Philip
  starts fresh from your revised request.

## 5. Confirmation

You'll see the task name and a link to it in Asana. The task is filed in the
**OGC Deacons** project, **Inbox** section. We also email you a receipt with
the same link. It's best-effort, so if it doesn't arrive, the task was still
created.

<Tip>
  **Want updates?** Open the task in Asana and click **Follow**. You'll need a
  free Asana account. The first time, Asana may ask you to **Request access**
  — a deacon will approve it. You aren't added as a follower automatically.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  If your attachment failed to upload, the confirmation screen says so. The
  **task itself was still created** — don't resubmit. Add the file directly to
  the Asana task, or email it to the office.
</Warning>
